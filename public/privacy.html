Privacy Policy

Effective date: August 10, 2026 · Last updated: September 28, 2026

Arbor is an end-to-end encrypted messenger. This policy explains what we can and cannot see, what we collect, who can read your messages, how long things are kept, and what we do with it. It is written to be honest about our limits. Arbor is designed so that we cannot read your messages, but a messaging service unavoidably processes some data to route and deliver them, and we explain that plainly below.

The service is operated by The Arbor Project ("Arbor", "we", "us"). Contact: contact@arborsecure.app.

1. The short version

We cannot see the content of your messages, calls, or attachments. They are end-to-end encrypted on your device, and the server only ever holds ciphertext it has no key for.

We can see some metadata needed to run the service: group and channel labels, who invited whom (the tree structure), message routing data (which encrypted envelope is addressed to which node, when, and how large it is), and a coarse "last active" day. Your display names, network names, profile photos, bios and name history are end-to-end encrypted — the server stores them only as ciphertext. The full list is in Section 2.

Other people read what you send them — that is the point. In a network whose owner has turned Monitoring on, members can also read the circle conversations of the people up to two levels below them, and receive copies of announcements made below them. Section 3 explains exactly who can read what.

No ads, no trackers, no analytics, no third-party scripts, no data sales. No email address or phone number is required to use Arbor. Your data is stored on servers located in Switzerland.

2. What we collect

Account information

  • Username, stored only as a keyed hash (HMAC) rather than in cleartext, so a database dump does not reveal who holds an account. You still type your username to log in.
  • Password verifier. We store a one-way scrypt hash of a value your device derives from your password. We never receive or store your actual password.
  • A random per-account salt used for key derivation.
  • Your settings (for example whether read receipts are on and which notifications you want).
  • No email, phone number, real name, or contact list is collected or required.

Message content, calls, and attachments

  • Encrypted on your device before transmission (Signal protocol: X3DH + Double Ratchet). We store only ciphertext and cannot decrypt it. Attachments are encrypted separately and stored as opaque blobs.
  • Calls are end-to-end encrypted. We relay only the call set-up signaling, which we cannot read (see "Calls and IP addresses" below for what a call does reveal).

Metadata we necessarily process

  • Encrypted profiles (we cannot read them): your display name, network names, profile photo, bio and name history are encrypted on your device with a key shared only among the members of that network, and your account's default profile with a key derived from your password. The server stores and relays the ciphertext and cannot read it. Photos are also stripped of hidden metadata (location, camera, timestamps) on your device before they are encrypted.
  • Membership certificates: small signed records (made with your devices' encryption identity keys) that let members' apps check who really belongs to a network. They contain opaque node identifiers and public keys, not names.
  • Group and channel labels you create, and which members are in them.
  • Interaction metadata: emoji reactions and announcement acknowledgments (which node reacted to or acknowledged which message — names are resolved on members' devices), which recipients a message @-mentions (to decide whether to send a push), and call signaling metadata (who is calling whom, and when — never the call itself). Read receipts are on by default and only exchanged when both people have them on; the server passes on which of the sender's messages you have read, and does not store that. You can turn them off in Settings.
  • Encrypted backups: your devices upload your encryption state and text history encrypted under a key derived from your password on your device (we cannot read it), and, if you set one up, your recovery-phrase blob, encrypted under a key derived from that phrase.
  • Tree structure: the invite relationships (who invited whom), used to route messages and enforce access. This is visible to the server; it cannot be hidden while the server enforces who may read what.
  • Message routing data: the encrypted envelope, its recipient node identifiers (opaque handles), its size, a timestamp, and any expiry. Needed to deliver messages to the right devices.
  • Scheduled messages: if you schedule a message, its encrypted envelope and the time to send it are stored until it is sent (at most 30 days ahead).
  • Coarse activity: at most a day-level "last seen" date per account (for example 2026-08-10), overwritten each day. No clock times, no per-message history, no browsing trail.
  • Public keys and prekeys for your nodes, so others can start encrypted sessions with you.

Technical and device data

  • Cookies and device storage: one essential session cookie keeps you signed in (7 days); there are no tracking cookies. The app keeps your keys and your message history on your device, with message content encrypted under a key derived from your password.
  • Push notifications (only if you enable them): a push subscription token, used to deliver notifications through your browser or OS push service (for example Google, Apple, Mozilla). Notifications about messages say only that something new arrived ("New secure transmission."), never who wrote it or what it says; the push service does learn that, and when, your device was notified.
  • IP addresses: our application keeps your IP address only in memory, for at most 15 minutes, to rate-limit abuse; it never writes it to our database or logs. Our hosting and network providers process connection data to serve traffic and mitigate abuse (Section 6).
  • Operational logs: the server logs errors and service events. They never contain message content, names, usernames or IP addresses; they can contain internal identifiers (for example a payment subscription id when a payment step fails).

Calls and IP addresses

  • To connect a call, your device and the other person's device exchange network addresses. When a direct connection is possible, the other person's device can see your IP address, and yours can see theirs.
  • Arbor doesn't relay calls yet (relayed calls are planned for Premium). When a direct connection isn't possible — for example when both networks block one — the call can't connect. While setting up a call your device also contacts a STUN server — ours, or a public one such as Google's — which sees your IP address.

Payments

  • If you pay for Premium (or donate), payment is handled by our processors, Stripe (card) and NOWPayments (Monero/crypto). We do not receive or store your card number or crypto wallet details. Their privacy policies govern that data. We keep the processor's customer, subscription, invoice and payment identifiers linked to your account and to the network being paid for, so renewals, cancellations and refunds are applied correctly, and the date until which that network is paid for.

3. Who can read your messages

Your device encrypts each message separately for each person allowed to read it, and the server checks that list before it stores anything. Who that is depends on the kind of conversation:

  • Circle chats in a network: the people in that circle — the person who invited you and the others they invited into the same group, or the people you invited.
  • Groups and linked channels: the members of that group or channel, including anyone the owner added as a visitor.
  • Global chat (on unless the network owner turned it off): every member of the network — and everyone in it can see each other's name, photo and bio. It doesn't connect members in any other way (no calls or direct messages across the network's walls).
  • Announcements: the people the sender chose (or everyone below them).
  • Personal hubs: only your accepted contacts. Direct-message networks: only the people you're directly connected to — the person who invited you and the people you invited.

Monitoring. A network's owner can turn on Monitoring (it is off when a network is created; networks created with an older version of Arbor have it on until the owner turns it off). While it is on:

  • members can read the circle conversations of the people up to two levels below them (the people they invited, and the people those invited) — their devices receive their own encrypted copy of those messages;
  • everyone above the sender of an announcement receives a copy of it.

Turning Monitoring off stops these copies for new messages. Copies already delivered stay with the people who received them.

Anyone who receives a message can keep it on their device, screenshot it or forward it. Arbor cannot prevent that.

4. How we use data

  • To operate the service: deliver encrypted messages, establish calls, and maintain your account and networks.
  • To secure the service: rate-limiting and a signup proof-of-work to deter automated abuse and spam.
  • To process payments you initiate and apply them to the right network.

We do not use your data for advertising, profiling, or sale, and we do not run third-party analytics or trackers.

5. Legal basis (EEA/UK)

Where the GDPR/UK GDPR applies, we process the limited data above to perform our contract with you (providing the service), on the basis of our legitimate interest in operating and securing it, and, for optional features like push, on your consent.

6. Who we share with

We do not sell or rent data. We share only with service providers strictly necessary to run Arbor:

  • Hosting: our cloud hosting provider, located in Switzerland, which stores and processes application data.
  • Network / CDN: Cloudflare, which routes and protects traffic to the service.
  • Payment processors: Stripe and NOWPayments, only if you pay or donate.
  • Push services: your browser or OS vendor's push infrastructure, only if you enable notifications.

Legal requests. If compelled by valid legal process, we can only produce what we actually hold: account metadata and ciphertext. Because of end-to-end encryption, we cannot produce the content of your messages, calls, or attachments, as we have no ability to decrypt them.

7. How long we keep things

  • Messages and attachments on the server: deleted 90 days after they were sent (365 days in Premium networks), whether or not every device has fetched them — or sooner if the sender set them to disappear or deletes them. Your devices keep their own copies until you delete them. If a network's Premium ends, its messages older than 90 days are then deleted from the server.
  • Reactions and acknowledgments are deleted with their message. A "delete for everyone" leaves a small marker (which message, and which of its recipients should remove it) for 7 days, so devices that were offline still remove it.
  • Sign-in sessions expire after 7 days. Push subscriptions are kept until you turn notifications off, the push service reports them expired, or you delete your account.
  • Account, network and profile data, encrypted backups and payment identifiers: kept until you delete them, the identity, the network or your account.
  • Activity day: overwritten daily, never accumulated into a history.
  • Our backups: the database is backed up nightly, encrypted, and each backup is kept for 14 days. Something you delete is erased from the live database immediately and is gone from our backups within 14 days.

8. Your choices and rights

  • Deleting your account: open Settings → Danger zone → Delete my account and confirm with your password. This erases your account record (hashed username, password verifier, salt, settings, payment identifiers), every identity it holds, the messages you sent that are still on the server, your reactions and acknowledgments, your encrypted backups, recovery blob, push subscriptions, profile photos and bios, and cancels any card subscription (if our card processor can't be reached at that moment, we keep retrying for up to 30 days). People you invited stay in their networks. If you own a network other people are in, you are asked to confirm that it will be deleted for everyone. Copies of your messages already on other people's devices stay there.
  • Deleting a network: a network's owner can delete it (and everything in it on the server) from Settings.
  • Other rights: depending on your location you may have rights to access, correct, delete, or port your data; contact us to exercise them.
  • Important limit: because of end-to-end encryption and our zero-knowledge design, we cannot recover your account content if you lose your password and recovery phrase, and we cannot decrypt your messages for you or anyone else.

9. Security

Message content is end-to-end encrypted, private keys never leave your device in usable form, usernames are stored hashed, and we minimize the metadata we hold. No system is perfectly secure, and we provide the service without warranty. We do not claim it is unbreakable.

10. Children

Arbor is not directed to children. You must be at least 13 years old to use it, and we do not knowingly collect personal data from children under 13. Because Arbor is end-to-end encrypted, neither we nor any automated filter can see or screen what is sent or received, so parents and guardians should supervise any minor who uses Arbor. If you believe a child under 13 has provided us data, contact us and we will delete it.

11. Where your data is processed

Arbor is operated from California, USA, while its application data is stored and processed on servers located in Switzerland, a jurisdiction with strong data-protection law (the Swiss FADP) that the EU recognizes as providing an adequate level of protection. By using Arbor you understand that your data will be processed in Switzerland, and that our providers may route traffic through other countries in the course of delivering the service.
