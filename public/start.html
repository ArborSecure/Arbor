Three shapes

One app, three ways to organize

Pick the structure when you create a network. The encryption boundaries follow the shape you choose.

Chain of command

A branching hierarchy for teams, organizations, and communities. Anyone you accept can invite people beneath them. You talk to the circle directly above and below you, send announcements down your tree, and oversee at most two levels down.

Best for: companies, field teams, communities with structure.

A private roster

Hub-and-spoke: only you invite people, and each person you accept talks one-to-one with you. Members never see or reach each other. It still presents as a named, branded space you control. Think of it as a contained space for direct messages you can use for different organizations or operations you host.

Best for: client work, sources, a roster you personally manage.

Just your chats

No structure at all, just your own space like any other messenger direct messages. Share a link or QR code, and anyone who joins gets a private one-to-one chat with you. It's the simplest way for people to reach you, fully encrypted. You get one of these per account.

Best for: friends, family, day-to-day messaging.

The idea

A network you can actually see the shape of

Every person is a node. You invite people beneath you, and they invite people beneath them. What you can read depends on where you sit in the tree, and Arbor backs that up by only ever encrypting a message for the people who are allowed to read it.

Why Arbor

What a flat messenger can't do

Boundaries by encryption

Visibility isn't a toggle in an admin panel that someone can misconfigure. A message is only ever encrypted for the people allowed to read it, and everyone else is left out by the math itself.

Oversight that has limits

Leaders can see two levels below them for accountability, and no further. That cap applies to the founder too. You can also turn it off completely for a network with no monitoring at all.

The server sees nothing

No plaintext, no keys, no readable metadata. Node identities are opaque, attachments and calls are sealed client-side. If our servers were seized tomorrow, they'd hold only ciphertext.

Groups without overlap

Sort the people you invited into groups. Each group gets its own isolated chat. Nobody crosses over, and that separation is enforced on the server, not just hidden in your app.

Encrypted voice & video

Calls run peer-to-peer with keys exchanged over the same ratchet as your messages. The signaling relay never sees the media or the keys.

Yours to keep

You hold a 12-word recovery phrase, not us. Verify contacts with safety numbers, get warned the moment a key changes, and wipe everything with a panic button.

The security model, plainly

We built it so we can't betray you

Encryption keys are derived from your passphrase in your browser and never leave it. Messages are sealed with the Signal protocol, the same X3DH handshake and Double Ratchet that security researchers point to as the standard.

Trust, earned

Don't trust us. Verify us.

Security you have to take on faith isn't security. Everything about Arbor is built so you don't have to believe a word we say. You can check it yourself, or have someone you trust check it for you.

Read every line

Arbor is open source under the GPL. The exact code that runs is public. Read it yourself, or hand it to a security expert. There's nothing hidden.

The server can't cheat

We're not asking you to trust that we won't read your messages. We can't. No plaintext, no keys, opaque identities. The architecture makes betrayal impossible, not just against policy.

Verify who you're talking to

Confirm any contact with a safety number, and get warned the instant a key changes. You never have to trust that the person on the other end is who they claim to be. You can prove it.

Break it, tell us

Found a flaw? We want to hear it. Responsible disclosure at bugreports@arborsecure.app. Security is an ongoing process, done out in the open, not a one-time claim.

We'd genuinely rather you check than take our word for it. That's the whole point.

About

A passion project to change how we talk

Arbor started with a simple frustration. Every messenger is either a flat list of contacts or one sprawling group chat, and nearly all of them are quietly watching you. Private, structured communication should not be this hard.

So I built the messenger I wanted to exist. One where your network has a real shape, where who can see what is decided by math instead of a settings toggle someone can get wrong, and where the people running the servers genuinely cannot read a word you send.

It has been an ongoing project for years, built and paid for out of my own pocket, hosting and all. It is open-source so anyone can verify it, and it always will be. There is no company behind it and no shareholders to please. The only thing keeping it running is people who believe in it.

If that is you, thank you for being here.

Honest disclosures

What you should know

You hold the keys, and the responsibility

Your encryption keys are derived from your passphrase and stored only on your devices. We can't reset your password or recover your account for you. Your 12-word recovery phrase is the only backup, so write it down and keep it somewhere safe. If you lose it and your devices, that data is gone. That's the trade-off that makes end-to-end encryption real.

Oversight is a feature, so be upfront about it

In hierarchical networks, people above you can read up to two levels of the conversations below them, for accountability. This is by design and is disclosed here so no one is surprised. Founders can disable monitoring entirely. If you run a network, tell your members how it's configured.
